# Recipe website open card sort

A ready-to-run open card sort for a recipe and cooking website. Participants group 30 pieces of content however makes sense to them and name each group, which shows you how home cooks naturally organise recipes, guides and tips.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Study type | Card sort (open) |
| Tests | How people group and name recipe-site content |
| Cards | 30 |
| Suggested participants | 15 to 30 |
| Time per participant | About 10 to 15 minutes |

## When to use it

Use an open sort early, before you've decided on a structure. Recipe content can be grouped in many ways (by meal, ingredient, diet, occasion, skill or time) and an open sort shows which of these people reach for first. The cards mix recipes with guides and techniques on purpose, to see whether people want them kept together or apart.

## How to run it in tilia

1. Download this file.
2. In tilia, choose **+ New study**, then **Card sort**, and select **Open sort**.
3. Click **import a markdown file** and upload this file. tilia reads only the cards below and ignores the rest.
4. Turn on random card order in the study's **Settings** tab, so cards near the top of the list don't get sorted more carefully than the rest.
5. Publish the study and share the link with participants.

## Adapting it

Replace the cards with your own content, using real page titles where you can. Aim for 30 to 50 cards: fewer gives you little to learn from, more and people start sorting carelessly. Leave out cards that only make sense if you already know your site.

## Reading the results

Look for cards that most people put together, and for the names people give their groups. Those names are candidates for your navigation labels. Cards that end up in lots of different groups are worth a closer look: they might belong in more than one place, or their title might be unclear.
