# Help centre tree test

A ready-to-run tree test for a software product's help centre. It checks whether customers can find the right article for common questions about setup, billing, security and troubleshooting, using a typical project management tool as the example.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Study type | Tree test |
| Tests | Help centre categories and article placement |
| Tasks | 7 |
| Suggested participants | 30 to 50 |
| Time per participant | About 5 minutes |

## When to use it

Use it when support tickets suggest people aren't finding answers that already exist, or before reorganising a help centre that has grown one article at a time. Help centres often mirror the product team's structure. This test shows whether that structure matches how customers describe their problems.

## How to run it in tilia

1. Download this file.
2. In tilia, choose **+ New study**, then **Tree testing**, and click **import a markdown file**.
3. Upload this file. tilia reads only the tree below and ignores the rest.
4. Add the tasks below under **Tasks**, choosing each correct answer in the tree.
5. Publish the study and share the link with participants.

## The tree

- Help centre
  - Getting started
    - Create your account
    - Set up your first project
    - Invite your team
    - Import from another tool
  - Projects and tasks
    - Create and assign tasks
    - Due dates and reminders
    - Templates
    - Archive or delete a project
  - Collaboration
    - Comments and mentions
    - Sharing with guests
    - Notifications
  - Integrations
    - Slack
    - Google Drive
    - Calendar sync
    - API and webhooks
  - Billing and plans
    - Compare plans
    - Change your plan
    - Invoices and receipts
    - Cancel your subscription
  - Account and security
    - Reset your password
    - Two-factor authentication
    - Single sign-on (SSO)
    - Delete your account
  - Troubleshooting
    - Can't log in
    - Email notifications not arriving
    - Known issues
    - Contact support

## Tasks

Each task describes a goal without using the words in the tree, so participants have to think about where things belong rather than match words.

1. Your finance team needs a copy of last month's bill for their records. Where would you find it?
   Correct answer: Billing and plans › Invoices and receipts
2. A freelance designer outside your company needs to see one project, without joining your whole workspace. Where would you find out how?
   Correct answer: Collaboration › Sharing with guests
3. Your team has been tracking work in spreadsheets and wants to bring it all across. Where would you look?
   Correct answer: Getting started › Import from another tool
4. Your IT department wants everyone to log in with their work Microsoft account. Where would you find out if that's possible?
   Correct answer: Account and security › Single sign-on (SSO)
5. You used to get an email whenever someone replied to you, but they've stopped. Where would you look?
   Correct answers: Troubleshooting › Email notifications not arriving, or Collaboration › Notifications
6. A developer on your team wants new tasks to appear automatically in an internal dashboard. Where would you send them?
   Correct answer: Integrations › API and webhooks
7. You've forgotten how you sign in and you're locked out. Where would you go?
   Correct answers: Account and security › Reset your password, or Troubleshooting › Can't log in

## Adapting it

Replace the categories and article titles with your own help centre's, keeping the depth roughly the same. Write tasks from real support tickets, in your customers' words rather than your product's. Keep tasks to 10 or fewer and turn on random task order in the study's **Settings** tab.

Tasks 5 and 7 have two correct answers on purpose: help centres often have a "how it works" article and a "fix it" article for the same thing. If a task could reasonably be answered in two places, mark both as correct.

## Reading the results

tilia shows a success rate for each task: the share of participants who chose a correct answer. It also splits results into direct success (they went straight to the answer) and indirect success (they got there after going back up the tree). If people often end up in Troubleshooting for tasks that belong elsewhere, it may be acting as a catch-all, which is a sign the other categories aren't clear.
