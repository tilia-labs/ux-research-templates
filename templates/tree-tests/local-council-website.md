# Local council website tree test

A ready-to-run tree test for a local council website. It checks whether residents can find everyday services, from bin collections to Council Tax discounts, in a typical UK council's navigation.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Study type | Tree test |
| Tests | Navigation for waste, Council Tax, housing, roads, planning, schools and care |
| Tasks | 7 |
| Suggested participants | 30 to 50 |
| Time per participant | About 5 minutes |

## When to use it

Use it when you're restructuring a public service website, or to check that services named by the council's departments make sense to the residents who use them. Council websites serve almost everyone, so recruit a broad mix of ages and include people who rarely contact the council.

## How to run it in tilia

1. Download this file.
2. In tilia, choose **+ New study**, then **Tree testing**, and click **import a markdown file**.
3. Upload this file. tilia reads only the tree below and ignores the rest.
4. Add the tasks below under **Tasks**, choosing each correct answer in the tree.
5. Publish the study and share the link with participants.

## Tasks

Each task describes a goal without using the words in the tree, so participants have to think about where things belong rather than match words.

1. You've bought a new sofa and need the old one taken away. Where would you arrange this?
   Correct answer: Bins and recycling › Bulky waste collection
2. You live on your own and a friend mentioned you might be able to pay less to the council each year. Where would you check?
   Correct answer: Council Tax › Discounts and exemptions
3. A deep hole in the road outside your house damaged your car tyre. Where would you let the council know?
   Correct answer: Roads and parking › Report a pothole
4. Your neighbour is building an extension and you want to see the drawings they submitted. Where would you look?
   Correct answer: Planning and building › View planning applications
5. Your daughter starts primary school next September. Where would you go to get her a place?
   Correct answer: Schools and education › School admissions
6. You've just moved to the area and want to make sure you can vote in the next election. Where would you go?
   Correct answer: Your council › Elections and voting
7. Your dad is coming home from hospital and will need help with washing and meals. Where would you look?
   Correct answer: Adult social care › Care at home

## Adapting it

Swap the labels for your own council's navigation, keeping the depth roughly the same. Base tasks on the services residents use most, which you can usually find in your site's search terms or contact centre data. Keep tasks to 10 or fewer and turn on random task order in the study's **Settings** tab.

If a task could reasonably be answered in two places, mark both as correct. A tree test measures whether people can find things, not whether they pick the one place you had in mind.

## Reading the results

tilia shows a success rate for each task: the share of participants who chose a correct answer. It also splits results into direct success (they went straight to the answer) and indirect success (they got there after going back up the tree). Low success on a task usually means the service sits under a label that makes sense inside the council but not to residents.
