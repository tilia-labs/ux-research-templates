# GP practice website tree test

A ready-to-run tree test for a GP practice website. It checks whether patients can find the things they come for, like booking an appointment, ordering a repeat prescription or getting a sick note, when they're often unwell, worried or in a hurry.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Study type | Tree test |
| Tests | Navigation for appointments, prescriptions, test results, services, health advice and practice information |
| Tasks | 7 |
| Suggested participants | 30 to 50 |
| Time per participant | About 5 minutes |

## When to use it

Use it when reception is fielding calls that the website should answer, or before you rebuild or reorganise a practice site. GP sites tend to grow one notice at a time, so urgent tasks end up buried next to opening hours and newsletters. This test shows whether patients can find the right page without help.

Include people who aren't confident online, and people who use the site on a phone. Both are common among patients.

## Tasks

Each task describes a goal without using the words in the tree, so participants have to think about where things belong rather than match words.

1. You've had a cough for three weeks and want to see a doctor. Where would you go?
   Correct answer: Appointments › Book an appointment
2. You take the same tablets every month and are nearly out. Where would you go to get more?
   Correct answer: Prescriptions › Order a repeat prescription
3. You had bloods taken last week and want to know if the results are back. Where would you look?
   Correct answer: Test results › Blood test results
4. Your employer wants proof that you were too ill to work last week. Where would you go?
   Correct answer: Forms and letters › Sick notes and fit notes
5. You're travelling to Kenya in three months and want to know what jabs you need. Where would you go?
   Correct answer: Services › Travel health
6. It's Saturday night and your child has a high temperature that won't come down. Where would you go?
   Correct answers: Health advice › Out of hours and NHS 111, or Health advice › Emergency help
7. You've just moved to the area and want to see a doctor here. Where would you go?
   Correct answer: Your practice › Join the practice

## Adapting it

Swap the labels for your own site's navigation, keeping the depth roughly the same. Base tasks on what reception is asked most often. Keep tasks to 10 or fewer and turn on random task order in the study's **Settings** tab.

Task 6 has two correct answers on purpose. A worried parent could reasonably choose either page, and both lead them to safe next steps. A tree test measures whether people can find help, not whether they pick the one place you had in mind.

## Reading the results

tilia shows a success rate for each task: the share of participants who chose a correct answer. It also splits results into direct success (they went straight to the answer) and indirect success (they got there after going back up the tree). Watch tasks 1, 2 and 6 closely. A low direct success rate on booking, repeat prescriptions or urgent help usually means more phone calls, and for urgent help it can mean a delay that matters.

If many people look for test results under Appointments, or sick notes under Services, consider a short link on the home page to the most-searched tasks.
